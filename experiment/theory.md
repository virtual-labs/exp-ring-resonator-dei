A ring resonator is a microwave resonant structure formed by bending a transmission line into a closed loop. It is usually fabricated using microstrip or stripline technology on a dielectric substrate. A feed line is placed close to the ring with a small coupling gap that allows electromagnetic energy to transfer into the ring without a direct electrical connection.

The operating principle of a ring resonator is based on resonance. When the circumference of the ring becomes equal to an integer multiple of the guided wavelength the electromagnetic waves traveling around the ring reinforce each other. At this condition the stored electromagnetic energy reaches a maximum value which produces a strong resonant response.

The resonant condition is given by
L=nλ<sub>g</Sub>
where, L = circumference of the ring, n = integer mode number and λ<sub>g</Sub>= guided wavelength

The guided wavelength is related to the resonant frequency by

λ<sub>g</Sub>=c/fεeff, where c = speed of light, f = resonant frequency and εeff= effective dielectric constant

The resonant frequency depends on the ring circumference the dielectric constant of the substrate the conductor dimensions the coupling gap. Increasing the ring circumference lowers the resonant frequency while increasing the dielectric constant also reduces the resonant frequency.

A ring resonator has a high quality factor which indicates low energy loss with sharp resonance. The coupling gap controls the amount of power transferred from the feed line to the ring. Proper coupling improves the performance of the resonator while excessive coupling increases losses.

Ring resonators are widely used in microwave filters oscillators frequency selective circuits sensors dielectric constant measurement wireless communication systems RF integrated circuits. Their compact size high selectivity simple structure make them suitable for modern microwave applications.

####Advantages

Compact size
High quality factor
High frequency selectivity
Low radiation loss
Easy integration with microwave circuits

####Disadvantages

Sensitive to fabrication errors
Performance depends on substrate properties
Coupling gap requires accurate design
Losses increase at very high frequencies
