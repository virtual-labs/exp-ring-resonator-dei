What the animation shows: one RF cycle played in slow motion. The ring’s n = 1 standing wave pulses in place while energy swaps between the electric and magnetic field: when E is at its peak, H is zero, and a quarter-cycle later the roles reverse. On the feed lines the wave advances toward the ring (Port 1) and away from it (Port 2). Nothing is randomly moving or decorative.

What to Observe

Energy crosses the gapThe feed line never touches the ring. Energy is coupled across the gaps by the fringing electric field. Watch the red arrows in the gaps reverse direction as the signal alternates.
The response peaks at resonanceSweep the slider through fres: the ring field grows to its maximum, then falls again, and the phase swings from about +90° to −90°.
E and H are different picturesE is strongest at the ports (voltage antinodes), H is strongest at the sides (current antinodes). When one is at its peak, the other is zero.

This is a qualitative analytical visualization intended for conceptual learning. It is not a full-wave electromagnetic field solver and does not represent experimentally measured field distributions.

Model and assumptions
  • Single-mode (n = 1) ring. The ideal one-wavelength condition (mean circumference = one guided wavelength) holds at the unperturbed fundamental f1. The coupled resonance fres, which the field response is centred on, is shifted slightly below f1 by the analytical gap-loading model. f1, fres, QL, Qe and the geometry are the existing calculated values; nothing is recalculated here.
  • Ring field as a standing wave, φ measured clockwise from Port 1: V = A·cos φ·cos θ and I = A·sin φ·sin θ. V and I are 90° apart in space and time, so electric and magnetic energy alternate. Both are normalized to 1 at the ring peak at fres.
  • Frequency response: x = 2QL(f − fres)/fres, amplitude A = 1/√(1 + x²), phase ψ = −atan x. This is the same QL-based expression the S21 readout uses, so ψ is the phase of the S21 response and is 0° at fres.
  • Feed lines: Port 1 carries an incident wave of fixed reference amplitude 0.5 travelling toward the ring; Port 2 carries a wave of amplitude 0.5·|S21| travelling away. The guided wavelength on the feed uses the existing microstrip effective permittivity. Reflected waves are not drawn. Ring-to-feed levels are illustrative.
  • Gap field: E ≈ ΔV/g, where ΔV is the instantaneous potential difference between the feed end and the ring edge. Its brightness uses a square-root dependence on g so narrow gaps do not saturate the display.
  • Fringing: outside the strip edge the E magnitude decays as exp(−s/0.5h) and the H magnitude as exp(−s/1.5h), because electric flux is concentrated under the strip while magnetic flux spreads wider. These decay lengths are qualitative.
  • Colours encode |field| on a linear scale from 0 to the ring peak at fres (values above 1 are clipped). E uses the warm scale, H the teal scale. Polarity is shown by the vector symbols: ⊗ = E into the substrate (strip positive), ⊙ = E out of it.
  • Drawing: the gap is drawn at least 6 px wide and feed lines are drawn up to 20 mm long, so very small gaps or very long feeds are not to scale.
