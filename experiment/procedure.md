1. Click the “Concept” tab to read the basic theory of the ring resonator and its comparison with the linear λ/2 resonator.
2. Click the “Theory” tab to note down the resonant frequency, Q-factor, and equivalent circuit formulas.
3. Click the “Why 50 Ω?” tab (optional) to understand why 50 Ω is used as the standard reference impedance.
4. Click the “Design & Simulation” tab to open the design and simulation dashboard.
5. Enter the Geometry parameters (Ring Radius R, Strip Width W, Coupling Gap g, Feed Line Width Wf, Feed Line Length Lf) in the Design Parameters panel.
6. Enter the Substrate parameters (relative permittivity εr, substrate height h, loss tangent tanδ) in the same panel.
7. Observe the ring layout updating live in the Geometry Preview panel, and check the Calculated Results panel for f0, Z0, QL, and bandwidth.
8. Set the frequency sweep range (f_start, f_stop) and number of points in the Frequency Sweep section.
9. Click the “Run Simulation” button to perform the S-parameter sweep.
10. Observe the S-Parameters Magnitude, VSWR, and Group Delay graphs under the “S-Parameters” results tab.
11. Observe the standing wave pattern |V(z)| along the ring.
12. Click the “Equivalent Circuit” results tab to view the R_eq, L_eq, C_eq, and C_gap values with the circuit diagram.
13. Click the “Field Distribution” results tab, then move the frequency slider (or use the Play button) to observe the coupled field amplitude around the ring at different frequencies.
14. Change one design parameter at a time and re-run the simulation to observe its effect on resonant frequency and Q-factor.
15. Record all observations and write a conclusion based on the results obtained.
